## Absolute Ban Violations

### 1. Gradient text — 7+ application points
`background-clip: text` with a gradient appears on:
- Hero title "Design." and "Motion." (`hero.jsx:14,16`)
- Pillars heading "One studio." (`sections.jsx:136`)
- Pillar h3s "Design", "Code", "Motion" (`sections.jsx:145`)
- Service card taglines `.svc-tagline` (`index.html:515`)
- Process step numbers `.pn` (`index.html:588`)

The brand allows gradient accents on distinct brand marks (logo, wordmark). Seven decorative text applications dilute the three-beam visual language and read as an AI design signature.

### 2. Eyebrow on every section — 6/6 sections
`.eyebrow` (12px mono uppercase, `--fg-faint` color, gradient rule) appears on:
Hero, Services, Pillars, Work, Process, CTA. When every section opens with the same pattern it stops being hierarchy and becomes wallpaper. It also sets up a contrast violation on every one of these labels.

### 3. Numbered scaffold markers
Process `01`–`04` step numbers: the steps are a genuine sequence — this is the one context where numbering earns its place. Pillar indices `01 / 03` inside the pillar cards are scaffolding numbers with no sequential information value.

## Priority Issues

### P0 — Blocks conversion

**No contact mechanism**
Both CTA section buttons (`href="#"`, `sections.jsx:232–233`) and the hero "Start a project" button (`hero.jsx:24`) do nothing. A landing page whose entire purpose is to generate enquiries has no way to receive them. Fix: add a Typeform/Tally embed, Calendly link, or `mailto:` before launch.

**No mobile navigation**
`@media (max-width: 880px)` hides all `.nav-link` elements with no burger replacement (index.html:275–277). Mobile visitors see a logo and a "Start a project" CTA that goes nowhere. Half the page's sections are unreachable without knowing to scroll blindly. Fix: add a hamburger that toggles a mobile nav overlay.

**In-browser Babel transpilation**
The page loads React 18.3.1 + Babel standalone + Three.js from CDN — roughly 1.4MB+ of unminified JS that must be fetched, parsed, and compiled before any React component renders. On a mid-range 4G connection, time-to-interactive exceeds 8 seconds. This directly impacts the conversion rate the page is designed to maximize. Fix: pre-compile JSX to plain JS (esbuild, Vite, or even a one-time Babel CLI pass), drop Babel standalone from the bundle.

---

### P1 — Damages credibility or accessibility

**`--fg-faint` fails WCAG AA contrast**
Computed value on dark background: `rgba(243,242,248,.40)` on `#07070c` → approximately #65656A → **3.46:1**. Required: 4.5:1 for normal text. Applied to eyebrow labels (12px), pillar index badges (12px), and pillar sub-numbers (14px normal weight). Fix: raise opacity to ~0.55 or use a fixed color near `#8a8899`.

**Gradient text on 7+ elements (absolute ban, legibility impact)**
Beyond the craft violation, gradient text creates a contrast ambiguity: the lightest part of the gradient (`--a3: #ff4d9d`) on a dark background reads ~3.2:1. Fix: restrict gradient text to three places maximum — logo wordmark, hero display title, and one architectural accent. Replace service taglines and process numbers with solid `--a1` or `--fg`.

**Eyebrows on all 6 sections (absolute ban)**
Remove at least 4 of 6. Keep the hero eyebrow (it establishes genre) and one other at most (CTA "Let's talk" works as a signal shift before the conversion block). Replace the remaining four with typographic variation — a lighter weight, a smaller size, an inline rule, or nothing.

**Placeholder trust and work content**
Trust logos are fictional business names; all three work cards have `data-ph="Replace with project shot"`. For a studio whose proposition is "we make you look premium," empty portfolio slots and invented clients actively undermine the pitch. This is the largest credibility gap. Fix before launch: use two real or clearly labeled case studies; replace trust logos with real marks or remove the section.

---

### P2 — Degrades quality

**Hero title max 168px (10.5rem)**
`clamp(56px, 11.5vw, 168px)` exceeds the 6rem ceiling. At 1280px viewport, "Design." and "Motion." at 168px are typographically monumental but uncontrolled. Check for line break behavior at 900–1100px widths; the stacked layout likely wraps awkwardly. Fix: cap at `clamp(56px, 11vw, 96px)` for centered variant and test at 880–1100px.

**Em dashes in copy**
Three instances: "single goal — turning visitors into enquiries" (services), "goals and audience — then scope the smallest thing" (process), "a fair price — usually within two working days" (CTA). Replace with colons or rewrite the clause.

**`transition: padding` on nav (line 214)**
The `.nav.scrolled` padding shrink animates a layout property. Use `transform: translateY` on an inner wrapper or a fixed `height` property (which composites on some browsers) with a `padding` fallback that does not transition.

**`transition: width` on pillar barfill (line 540)**
`.pillar:hover .barfill` expands from `width: 46px` to `width: 100px`. Replace with `transform: scaleX(2.17)` + `transform-origin: left center`. Identical visual result, zero layout recalculation.

---

### P3 — Minor

- `line-height: 0.98` on h1–h3 is appropriate for display-only single-line heads but will collide on multi-line medium headings (h3 at section level). Consider `line-height: 1.05` for h3.
- No `<meta name="theme-color">` — mobile Chrome won't tint its address bar to match the dark bg.
- Footer social/email links all `href="#"` — not blockers, but every dead link chips away at trustworthiness.
- `.eyebrow::before` gradient rule is part of the eyebrow pattern and shares the contrast problem of `--fg-faint`.
- Service card number badges (`.svc-num`) sit adjacent to the icon in the card header — verify this doesn't read as a list-item ordinal; if it does, remove them (service card numbering is scaffolding).

## Minor Observations

- Body `font-size: 17px` and `line-height: 1.55` are both excellent for readability.
- `text-wrap: balance` on h1–h3 and `text-wrap: pretty` on `p` are exactly right.
- `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` block exists — verify it covers the Three.js canvas animation (orb/reef particle motion).
- Scroll-reveal `data-reveal` system: confirm that elements with `data-reveal` are visible without the class, so content isn't gated on an animation firing in a headless context.
- The tweaks panel is a nice demo differentiator but may confuse real clients on the live page — consider hiding it behind a keyboard shortcut or removing it from the production build.
- `color-mix(in oklab, ...)` for nav backdrop and button shadows is a good modern approach; verify browser support floor (Firefox 113+, Chrome 111+, Safari 16.2+).
